01 · Literature Survey

Literature Survey

Prior work reviewed for each layer of SOHOWALL.

SOHO networks face the same commodity threats as enterprises (malware, phishing, botnet C2, data leakage, misconfiguration) but lack the expertise, budget and hardware that enterprise defences assume. The literature covers threat-intelligence quality and reputation systems, ML-based anomaly detection, privacy-preserving data loss prevention, and NLP for security-policy management. Most of it targets enterprise or ISP environments.

M1

Threat intelligence

  • Feed quality studies show that public feeds overlap little and vary in timeliness: Li et al. (2019), Griffioen et al. (2020), Bouwman et al. (2020).
  • Reputation systems such as Notos (2010) and Exposure (2011) need ISP-scale passive DNS. BLAG (2020) improves blocklist accuracy.
  • MISP (Wagner et al., 2016) supports sharing, and Pi-hole applies static lists at home. Neither grades indicators or enforces safely.
M2

Network anomaly detection

  • Autoencoders trained only on normal traffic flag anomalies by their reconstruction error: Sakurada & Yairi (2014).
  • Kitsune (Mirsky et al., 2018) showed that online autoencoder ensembles can detect network intrusions on modest hardware.
  • TinyML (Warden & Situnayake, 2019) makes compact models practical on low-power devices. Sommer & Paxson (2010) warn about how ML-based detection is evaluated.
M3

Privacy-preserving data loss prevention

  • DLP surveys find that most systems rely on keyword and regular-expression matching with little context: Alneyadi et al. (2016).
  • Transformer models such as BERT (Devlin et al., 2019) enable context-aware recognition of personal data.
  • Fully homomorphic encryption (Gentry, 2009) and the BFV scheme (Fan & Vercauteren, 2012), implemented in Microsoft SEAL, allow computation on encrypted values.
M4

NLP for security-policy management

  • Firewall misconfiguration is common even among professionals: Wool (2004). NIST SP 800-41 sets out firewall policy guidance.
  • DistilBERT (Sanh et al., 2019) provides compact text classification, and Flan-T5 (Chung et al., 2024) instruction-tuned text generation.
  • LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) fine-tunes large models by training only a small number of added parameters.

02 · Research Gap

Research Gap

What existing work does not yet cover, per module.

Existing SOHO protections are single-layer and signature-based. They can't handle low-quality threat intelligence, unknown attacks, insider data leakage, or configuration mistakes by non-expert administrators.

ModuleExisting workLimitationSOHOWALL approach
M1 Feed blocklists, vendor severity ratings, ISP-scale reputation systems No objective severity, no coverage of unlisted hosts without big data, alerts with no safe automatic blocking Severity derived from ATT&CK, feed corroboration and freshness, a hostname-only ML fallback, guarded enforcement through M4
M2 Signature-based IDS (Snort, Suricata); ML-based NIDS such as Kitsune Signatures miss unknown attacks; many ML detectors assume enterprise hardware and analyst tuning Lightweight TinyML anomaly detection fed by M1's captured features
M3 Enterprise DLP suites; keyword and regular-expression PII matching Costly and centralised; pattern matching misses context; monitoring data and risk scores are kept in plain text Transformer PII detection with user risk scores kept under FHE (privacy-preserving)
M4 Manual iptables syntax, GUI front-ends and vendor configuration wizards Firewall syntax needs expertise; misconfiguration causes exposure and outages NLP intent and rule generation with validation, guardrails, an audit trail and one-step reversal
03 · Research Problem

Research Problem

Main research problem

SOHO users need enterprise-grade, intelligent, layered protection that runs on low-resource hardware and can be managed by non-experts.

Research questions
M1

Threat Intelligence & Enforcement

  1. Can MITRE ATT&CK be used to derive objective and reproducible severity weights for the threat categories in public feeds, and do they rank the categories plausibly?
  2. How effectively can a lightweight ensemble classify hostnames that no feed lists, using only lexical features, and is it reliable enough to act on automatically?
  3. Does the scoring and enforcement pipeline behave correctly and safely, never blocking local or safelisted addresses, and is it fast enough for a small network?
M2

Anomaly Detection

  1. Can an autoencoder trained only on normal traffic, with its threshold at the 95th percentile of validation reconstruction error, detect anomalous events with an F1-score of at least 0.90 while keeping the false-positive rate near 5%?
  2. Does reporting per-feature reconstruction error give an administrator a meaningful explanation of why an event was flagged?
  3. Can a DeBERTa-v2 model fine-tuned with LoRA detect PII in e-mail text with an F5-score of at least 0.90 while training only a small fraction of its parameters?
  4. Can both detection layers be served with acceptable latency (at most 500 ms per event) and memory use (at most 512 MB) for SOHO deployment?
M3

Data Loss Prevention

  1. How reliably can the token-classification model identify the PII classes relevant to the SOHO DLP policy?
  2. Does application-mediated text inspection stop outgoing e-mail that contains designated sensitive content while allowing benign e-mail?
  3. Can clipboard and screenshot signals be recorded and attributed accurately without collecting excessive sensitive data?
  4. Does homomorphic accumulation produce exact risk totals while keeping the ciphertext unreadable in the database?
  5. What performance and operational trade-offs arise compared with rule-only and non-encrypted baselines?
M4

Natural Language Firewall

  1. How accurately can DistilBERT identify firewall intentions (allow, block, restrict) from natural-language instructions?
  2. How effectively can Flan-T5 with PEFT adapters convert natural-language requests into syntactically valid and semantically appropriate iptables commands?
  3. To what extent do rule validation and the regex fallback reduce invalid or unsafe commands compared with ML generation alone?
  4. Can the component safely apply, record and reverse iptables rules and domain blocks while keeping unsafe commands away from the execution layer?
  5. How reliably can M4 process authorised block and unblock requests from the CTI component while rejecting unauthorised or unsafe ones?
04 · Research Objectives

Research Objectives

Main objective

Design, implement and evaluate a multi-layered, intelligent security solution that enables non-specialist owners of SOHO networks to detect external threats, prevent data leakage and enforce firewall policy safely and transparently.

Module objectives
M1

Threat Intelligence & Enforcement

Aim: Design, implement and evaluate a lightweight CTI component that turns public threat intelligence and locally captured network metadata into explainable risk scores, and uses them to block threats safely on SOHO networks.

  1. A capture pipeline for DNS, TLS SNI and TCP-flag metadata with direction labelling and a single write path.
  2. Integrate URLhaus, ThreatFox and Feodo Tracker, and map their tags to ATT&CK-based categories.
  3. Derive a reproducible severity weight per category from ATT&CK, combined with corroboration and freshness into one score.
  4. An ML ensemble that scores unlisted hostnames using only features available at decision time.
  5. Safe enforcement through local guards and a non-raising client for M4's API, plus live anomaly integration with M2.
  6. Evaluate the severity derivation, the fallback model, pipeline correctness and performance.
M2

Anomaly Detection

Aim: Design, implement and evaluate a lightweight, explainable anomaly-detection component that finds previously unseen network behaviour with an autoencoder and personal data in e-mail with a fine-tuned transformer, integrated into SOHOWALL.

  1. Engineer a feature vector per captured event from windowed statistics, inter-arrival timing, protocol and TCP-flag indicators and FFT spectral features.
  2. Train an autoencoder on normal traffic only and select its decision threshold from clean validation data.
  3. Explain every decision with its reconstruction error, threshold, margin and top contributing features.
  4. Fine-tune DeBERTa-v2 with LoRA to detect seven PII entity types in e-mail, prioritising recall (F5), with regex validation.
  5. Package the model as a standalone microservice with health, scoring, statistics, history and simulation endpoints.
  6. Integrate with the SOHOWALL dashboard and evaluate detection quality, latency and resource use.
M3

Data Loss Prevention

Aim: Design, implement and evaluate a practical machine-learning-supported DLP component for SOHO use, focusing on detecting personal information in endpoint and application workflows, administrative visibility and protected risk-score accumulation.

  1. Review DLP approaches, contextual PII detection, endpoint versus gateway visibility and privacy-preserving monitoring.
  2. Implement collection and inspection pathways for selected e-mail, clipboard and screenshot events.
  3. Develop or integrate an entity-detection model whose labels match the scoring rules.
  4. Design event-based risk scoring and evaluate the correctness and threat model of homomorphic accumulation.
  5. Integrate an administrator view, client interface, authentication and event storage.
  6. Measure detection, blocking behaviour, latency, privacy exposure and integration reliability.
M4

Natural Language Firewall

Aim: Design, develop and evaluate a secure natural-language firewall rule generator and management API that lets SOHO administrators turn simple English instructions into validated firewall rules, while accepting automated requests from other SOHOWALL components.

  1. Classify firewall intent (allow, block, restrict) with DistilBERT, including several instructions in one request.
  2. Generate iptables commands with Flan-T5 and PEFT adapters.
  3. Validate generated rules, reject unsafe ones and fall back to deterministic regex parsing.
  4. Apply, record and reverse iptables rules and hosts-file domain blocks, with dry-run, safe file updates and an SQLite audit trail.
  5. Provide a token-authenticated REST API for the CTI component and evaluate functionality, security, response time and integration.
05 · Methodology

Methodology

Design science, iterative. M1 captures traffic metadata, scores it and asks M4 to enforce blocks over HTTP; M2 scores M1's traffic features for anomalies; M3 runs as a separate DLP layer; M4 writes the iptables and /etc/hosts rules.

ModuleData / inputMethodEvaluation
M1 Live DNS / TLS SNI / TCP-flag metadata; abuse.ch feeds; Malicious URLs dataset reduced to 193,597 unique hostnames (154,877 train / 38,720 test, split by host) ATT&CK v19.1 severity derivation; 7-model tree-ensemble fallback; guarded enforcement via M4 Fallback ROC-AUC 0.72; precision 0.905 and FPR 0.92% at the 0.6 threshold; 52 / 53 pytest tests pass; median local decision 57 ms
M2 Windowed statistical, timing, TCP-flag and FFT features sent from M1; synthetic e-mail text annotated with seven PII types TinyML autoencoder (16-8-16 hidden layers, notebook anomaly_detection_using_tiny_ml.ipynb) served by monitor.py; an event is anomalous when its reconstruction error (MSE) exceeds the 95th-percentile threshold; DeBERTa-v2 + LoRA e-mail PII detector F1 0.978 and ROC-AUC 0.999 on a 4,000-sample synthetic benchmark; 4.3% of 300 real normal records flagged; e-mail PII F5 0.904
M3 User activity on endpoints: clipboard, screenshots, outgoing e-mail and attachments, keyboard input and login attempts Transformer PII-detection and PII-type models; risk accumulated under FHE (Microsoft SEAL, BFV) DeBERTa-v3-small development experiment: F5 0.95, recall 0.96, precision 0.82. The deployed BERT model is not yet independently evaluated
M4 Plain-English admin commands; block/unblock requests from M1 DistilBERT intent classifier (allow / block / restrict); Flan-T5 + LoRA generator (0.45% trainable parameters); regex fallback; guardrails Intent accuracy, rule correctness, unsafe-rule rejection, reversal success: results TODO
